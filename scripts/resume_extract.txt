2. SELECTED CASE ù A PRODUCTION FAILURE WITH NO ERROR MESSAGE
I include this one because it is the clearest evidence of how I work, and because the difficulty of it is not visible from a resume bullet.
The presenting problem. Automate purchase-order approval routing in Salesforce. On its face, a routine configuration task.
The actual problem. Approval submissions were being cancelled roughly two seconds after creation. There was no error message,
no failed-record notification, and nothing in the standard system logs. From the userÆs perspective the submission simply did not
exist. The cause was a conflict between the internal approval process and protected background code inside the AvSight managed
package ù code that cannot be inspected or modified.
Diagnosis. Because standard logging showed nothing, I queried SalesforceÆs internal audit tables directly and analyzed the execution
timing patterns across submissions. The timing signature is what identified the conflict; nothing in the ordinary troubleshooting
surface would have surfaced it.
Three approaches evaluated and rejected
ò Direct modification of the conflicting logic ù blocked by managed-package restrictions. The code is sealed by the vendor.

--- page 2 ---
ò An alternative platform-native approach ù blocked by Salesforce platform limitations in that execution context.
ò A time-delay workaround ù rejected on business grounds before it was built. It would have appeared to work in testing. It
would also have failed intermittently once real users began editing PO line items after submission, producing an approval
process that mostly works and occasionally recalls a PO silently. In a procurement workflow that is an audit exposure, not an
inconvenience. This was the more important of the three rejections, because it was the one that would have shipped.
The solution
A Platform Event queue that decouples approval submission into a separate, clean transaction, removing the resource conflict
entirely. This is the same event-driven pattern used to resolve contention in payment processing and order management systems. It
was implemented declaratively ù no custom code, no changes to the vendor package, no dependence on undocumented platform
behavior ù and it scales with transaction volume without modification.
Time distribution. Roughly 90% of the effort went to understanding the problem and 10% to building the fix. That ratio is normal for
root-cause work and is the inverse of what a fast workaround produces: a quick resolution with an invisible liability underneath it.

4. TOWER ù CROSS-SYSTEM SEARCH PLATFORM
The recurring operational problem was not missing data; it was that answering a single business question required touching several
disconnected systems, and the time cost of that lookup was paid many times a day across the company.
I designed and built Tower, a search layer that branches across those systems and connects them into a single retrieval path, then
delivered successive iterations as usage patterns clarified what people were actually looking for.
Internally, the platform has been estimated at approximately $2M in annual business value. I want to be precise about that figure: it
is an internal estimate of time and efficiency recovered, not an audited financial result, and I present it as such.
5. DATA PROTECTION ù AWS BACKUP & RECOVERY ARCHITECTURE
SalesforceÆs native protections do not constitute a backup strategy, and the gap is easy to miss until it matters. I designed and
implemented a full backup architecture on AWS, along with the supporting infrastructure required for it to run reliably and
unattended.
ò Coverage extends beyond standard records to binary files and bulk datasets ù the categories most often left out of a nominal
backup and most painful to lose.
ò Retention model, data protection strategy, and recovery planning designed as one thing, since a backup with no tested recovery
path is a filing system.
ò Scoped external access: limited-permission AWS access shared with third parties where required, with dedicated integration
users rather than shared credentials.
